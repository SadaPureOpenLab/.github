SadaPure is a food and personal-care brand based in Lahore, Pakistan. Open Lab is where we build small, practical software that makes ingredient information easier to understand: see the full recipe, scale a quantity and check the assumptions behind a result.

## Multigrain Flour (Atta) Calculator

**[sadapure-flour-calculator](https://github.com/SadaPureOpenLab/sadapure-flour-calculator)** brings the published 18-ingredient blend, a 54-ingredient educational library and household planning into one codebase.

- Exact quantity scaling with a complete ingredient table and composition chart.
- Results update instantly when the goal or quantity changes, in a fixed dark interface.
- English and Urdu names, allergen flags, local CSV/PDF exports and printable results.
- 22 goal profiles with visible recipe provenance and linked guidance. Experimental percentages are not clinical doses.
- Official multigrain product button and a visitor-reviewed custom-order email draft.
- No accounts, trackers or stored customer or health profiles.

**[Open the live Flour (Atta) Builder](https://sadapureopenlab.github.io/sadapure-flour-calculator/)**

**Status:** public educational prototype. The initial GitHub build and deployment passed; 32 local automated checks cover the calculator, all 22 goals and order-draft privacy. Custom mixtures are food-development examples, not manufactured SadaPure products or clinically validated formulations.

## A transparent recipe starts with every ingredient

At 2.5 kg, the [published SadaPure ingredient table](https://sadapure.com/product/multi-grain-atta-flour) allocates 1,200 g to whole wheat, 250 g to barley, 200 g to oat flour and 150 g to kala chana flour. The calculator displays all 18 entries, including the small seed, spice and leaf amounts.

This is a public recipe snapshot retrieved on 14 October 2026, not a batch certificate. Older narrative copy differs from the percentage table; the discrepancy is documented rather than blended into the data. The original recipe contains wheat, barley, rye and sesame and is **not gluten-free**.

## Why a food brand publishes software

A percentage list is useful, but a household still has to turn it into grams. An ingredient name can be familiar in one language and unfamiliar in another. A calculation can look precise while hiding assumptions about portions or nutrition.

We build tools that make those details visible. Open code lets other people inspect the arithmetic, suggest clearer language and improve accessibility. It also makes the limits easier to see: software cannot establish clinical benefit, certify cross-contact controls or invent manufacturing records.

> Clear information is more useful than a claim a customer cannot check.

## Help improve the work

Developers can test calculation boundaries and exports. Designers can improve readable charts and mobile controls. Researchers and translators can correct ingredient identity, evidence and Urdu names with sources. Contributions should be specific, reproducible and free of customer or medical records.
